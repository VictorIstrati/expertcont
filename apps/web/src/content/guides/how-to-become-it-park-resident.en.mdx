Moldova IT Park (MITP) is, without question, **the most advantageous tax regime legally available in the Republic of Moldova for information technology companies**. The 7% single tax on sales revenue fully replaces a package of seven tax obligations: corporate income tax, personal income tax withheld from employee salaries, social contributions (CAS), health insurance (CNAM), local taxes, real-estate tax, and road duty.

For a mid-sized IT company, the annual difference compared to the standard regime consistently translates into **hundreds of thousands of MDL in savings** — money that can be reinvested directly into development, salaries, or international expansion.

That said, IT Park resident status is neither automatic nor obligation-free. Eligibility, the application process, the correct calculation of the single tax, monthly reporting, and the mandatory annual audit all require professional setup from day one. This guide walks through the full process end-to-end, with concrete 2026 figures, comparative calculation examples, and operational pitfalls identified in practice.

## What Moldova IT Park is

Unlike classic industrial parks, Moldova IT Park is **a virtual park**: residents are not required to operate from a specific physical location and can work from anywhere in the Republic of Moldova — or, under remote-work conditions, even from outside the country, provided economic substance requirements are met.

### Legal framework

The regime is governed by:
- **Law no. 77/2016 on information technology parks**
- **The Tax Code, arts. 367–377** (the single-tax regime)
- Subsequent normative acts issued by the MITP Administration

### Guaranteed duration

The preferential tax regime is guaranteed by law **until 2035**, and the Park's actual operation is secured **until 2037** (these terms were extended through successive amendments to Law no. 77/2016, with the most recent extension voted by Parliament in December 2023). Residents can request an extension of their contract until December 31, 2037 by signing an additional agreement on the MITP platform. This legislative guarantee is essential for companies planning medium- and long-term investments — Moldova IT Park is not a temporary facility but a structural component of national economic policy.

### What the 7% single tax replaces

For IT Park residents, the single tax paid monthly to the state budget includes (Tax Code art. 372):

- Corporate income tax (standard rate 12%)
- Personal income tax withheld from employee salaries (standard rate 12%)
- Social insurance contributions owed by the employer (24%)
- Employees' mandatory health insurance premiums (9%)
- Applicable local taxes
- Real-estate tax
- Road usage duty

**Outside the single tax remain**: VAT, the 6% tax withheld on dividend payments, taxes and contributions on non-salary payments (for example, payments to individuals or non-residents), and the park membership fee. Exports of IT services — services supplied to non-residents with the place of supply outside Moldova — are VAT-exempt with the right of deduction (0%, Tax Code art. 104(a)), which means that in practice the VAT burden is minimal.

## Eligibility: who can become a resident

### The legal-form criterion

Resident status is open to **legal entities and individuals registered in the Republic of Moldova as entrepreneurs** — LLC, JSC, but also sole proprietorships (ÎI) (Tax Code art. 368; [mitp.md](https://mitp.md/ro/de-ce-mitp/intrebari-frecvente/)). The entity must not be in insolvency, in liquidation, or have its activity suspended. For companies still being set up, see [the complete guide to opening an LLC in Moldova](/en/guides/guide-how-to-open-srl-moldova-2026).

### The eligible-activities criterion

At least **70% of sales revenue** must come from the eligible activities listed in art. 8 of Law no. 77/2016 (the full list, with CAEM codes, is on [mitp.md](https://mitp.md/ro/de-ce-mitp/conditiile-pentru-rezidenta/)). The list includes:

- Software development (web, mobile, desktop, embedded applications)
- IT services (consulting, integration, testing, maintenance, technical support)
- Web hosting and cloud services
- Specialized design (CAEM 74.10) and post-production for special effects, animation, and games
- Research and development in bioinformatics, nanobiotechnology, and other listed fields
- IT training (CAEM 85.59.12)
- Call-center and IT staffing services, for export only

The 70% threshold is calculated **monthly, cumulatively from the start of the year**. The law permits **no more than 2 calendar months per year** (Tax Code art. 368) in which eligible revenue may dip below 70%, provided that the cumulative annual average stays at or above 70%. In practice, we recommend monthly monitoring to avoid technical risk, but the legal threshold is annual with this grace margin.

### The economic-substance criterion

The law does not require a minimum number of employees — you can apply regardless of team size. We nevertheless recommend **real substance** in Moldova: employees with individual employment contracts, a demonstrable operational structure, and revenue that genuinely corresponds to the eligible activities, because these are checked annually.

### The per-employee threshold criterion

In practice, this is the most sensitive criterion. For each employee who worked or was on paid leave during the month — regardless of working hours — **the minimum single tax amount equals 30% of the projected national average monthly wage** (Tax Code arts. 369–370). For 2026, the projected average wage is MDL 17,400 (Government Decision no. 773/2025), so the minimum is **MDL 5,220 per month per employee**.

In practice, this minimum threshold applies as follows:

- The company calculates 7% of monthly sales revenue
- The company calculates the minimum amount = MDL 5,220 × number of employees
- **Pays the larger of the two**

## Calculating real savings: three comparative scenarios

To understand the real impact of the IT Park regime, we compare three typical scenarios with their equivalents under the standard tax regime.

Common assumptions: salaries are the only expense, employees do not use personal exemptions, and under the standard regime income tax is calculated after deducting the 9% health insurance premium (in 2026 employees no longer pay an individual social insurance contribution). The 6% dividend tax applies under both regimes, so it does not change the difference.

### Scenario 1: Small studio (3 employees, MDL 100,000/month in revenue)

**IT Park regime:**
- 7% × 100,000 = MDL 7,000
- Minimum amount: 3 × 5,220 = MDL 15,660
- **Monthly single tax: MDL 15,660** (the larger of the two)
- Effective rate: 15.66% of revenue

**Standard regime (gross salary MDL 30,000/month per employee):**
- Total gross salaries: MDL 90,000
- Employer CAS (24%): MDL 21,600
- Health insurance (9%): MDL 8,100
- Income tax (12% × 81,900): MDL 9,828
- Pre-tax profit: negative (100,000 − 90,000 − 21,600), so corporate income tax is 0
- **Total monthly tax obligations: MDL 39,528**

**Monthly savings via IT Park: MDL 23,868 → approximately MDL 286,000 annually**

### Scenario 2: Mid-sized company (10 employees, MDL 500,000/month in revenue)

**IT Park regime:**
- 7% × 500,000 = MDL 35,000
- Minimum amount: 10 × 5,220 = MDL 52,200
- **Monthly single tax: MDL 52,200**
- Effective rate: 10.44%

**Standard regime (gross salary MDL 35,000/month per employee):**
- Total gross salaries: MDL 350,000
- Employer CAS (24%): MDL 84,000
- Health insurance (9%): MDL 31,500
- Income tax (12% × 318,500): MDL 38,220
- Profit: 500,000 − 350,000 − 84,000 = MDL 66,000; corporate income tax (12%): MDL 7,920
- **Total monthly tax obligations: MDL 161,640**

**Monthly savings via IT Park: MDL 109,440 → approximately MDL 1,313,000 annually**

### Scenario 3: High-margin company (10 employees, MDL 2,000,000/month in revenue)

**IT Park regime:**
- 7% × 2,000,000 = MDL 140,000
- Minimum amount: 10 × 5,220 = MDL 52,200
- **Monthly single tax: MDL 140,000** (in this case, 7% exceeds the minimum threshold)
- Effective rate: 7%

**Standard regime (gross salary MDL 50,000/month per employee):**
- Total gross salaries: MDL 500,000
- Employer CAS (24%): MDL 120,000
- Health insurance (9%): MDL 45,000
- Income tax (12% × 455,000): MDL 54,600
- Profit: 2,000,000 − 500,000 − 120,000 = MDL 1,380,000; corporate income tax (12%): MDL 165,600
- **Total monthly tax obligations: MDL 385,200**

**Monthly savings via IT Park: MDL 245,200 → approximately MDL 2,942,000 annually**

You can rerun these calculations with your own figures in the [IT Park vs. standard regime calculator](/en/tools/it-park-calculator).

The mathematical conclusion is consistent: **the more profitable the company and the higher the salaries, the greater the savings under the IT Park regime**. For companies with thin margins or monthly revenue below the MDL 5,220 × number-of-employees threshold, the IT Park regime remains favorable, but the savings shrink noticeably.

## Stage 1: Pre-application preparation

Before applying for resident status, the company must be fully configured from a legal, accounting, and operational standpoint.

### Preliminary requirements

- **Entity registered in Moldova as an entrepreneur** (LLC, JSC, or ÎI)
- **CAEM-2 (Moldovan economic activity classifier) codes corresponding to eligible IT activities** — verify that the constitutive documents actually include the relevant codes; missing a code blocks classification of revenue as "IT"
- **Notification of existing employees** about the single-tax regime and its effects on social and health insurance, before registration in the park (art. 16(3) of Law no. 77/2016; templates are on [mitp.md](https://mitp.md/ro/cadrul-juridic-si-documentatie/))
- **Operational accounting**, with a system capable of monthly separation of revenue between eligible and ineligible activities
- **Minimal organizational structure**: appointed administrator, valid bylaws, validated registered office

### Bank-account decisions

We recommend opening, prior to application, **separate bank accounts** for:
- Revenue from eligible IT activities
- Revenue from ineligible activities (if any)
- The single tax (a dedicated account with the State Treasury — the details are communicated by the STS (State Tax Service) after registration)

This separation is not legally required but facilitates tracking the 70% threshold and preparing for audit.

## Stage 2: The application process

### The online platform

Applications are submitted exclusively online, via the **official Moldova IT Park platform** (administered by the MITP Administration). The process is fully digital and requires the electronic signature of the company's administrator.

### Required documents

- The resident registration request (electronic form)
- The updated bylaws of the company
- An extract from the State Register of Legal Entities
- Information about the activities carried out, detailing how they fit within the eligible domains
- Employee data (number, structure, employment contracts)
- Revenue and structure estimates for the application period

### Processing time

The MITP Administration examines a complete application **within at most 7 working days**; if the application is incomplete, the term is extended by up to 5 working days. After approval, the company has **30 calendar days** to sign the resident contract; otherwise, the application must be resubmitted. Resident status is obtained on the date the contract is signed ([mitp.md](https://mitp.md/ro/de-ce-mitp/intrebari-frecvente/)).

**Important**: the special tax regime applies **from the month following the one in which the contract was signed** (Tax Code art. 375(1)) — not retroactively. For example, a contract signed on 17 January means single tax from 1 February. For new companies, synchronizing incorporation with the IT Park application is essential.

### Costs

Residents pay a **monthly membership fee** to the MITP Administration: MDL 150 for the month in which status is obtained, then an amount calculated from estimated annual revenue, of at least MDL 150 per month, due by the 20th of each month (the first fee — within 5 working days of signing). The other costs are **consulting and preparation** (eligibility analysis, activity structuring, documentation drafting), which range from MDL 5,000 to MDL 25,000 depending on case complexity.

## Stage 3: Monthly obligations as a resident

### Calculating the single tax

Each month, the company computes:
1. **Total sales revenue** for the previous month
2. **7% of that revenue**
3. **The minimum amount** = MDL 5,220 × number of employees who worked or were on paid leave during the month
4. **The amount due = the larger of items 2 and 3**

### Reporting and payment deadlines

- **Declaration**: by the 25th of the following month
- **Payment**: by the 25th of the following month (same date)
- **Reporting via the STS system** — **Form IU17** (single-tax declaration); employee salary information is reported via the ISAPTI17 information note (Ministry of Finance Order no. 135/2017)
- **Semi-annual reports to the MITP Administration**: by July 25 (first half-year) and January 25 (second half-year), plus estimated revenue for the following year by December 31

### Employee salaries

Unlike the standard regime, **employees of IT Park residents receive their full gross salary as net salary** — with no CAS, CNAM, or income tax withholdings. The company pays all of those obligations via the single tax.

This peculiarity has two practical consequences:

1. **Talent attractiveness**: at the same cost to the company, the employee takes home a significantly larger net amount than under the standard regime
2. **Administrative simplification**: salary computation and payroll register issuance become much simpler

In months when the single tax applies, employees cannot benefit from the personal exemptions under Tax Code arts. 33–35.

### Monitoring the 70% threshold

One of the most sensitive obligations is monitoring **the proportion of revenue from eligible activities**. The 70% threshold applies **cumulatively over the calendar year**, with the legal grace of up to 2 months per year in which the proportion may dip below 70%, as long as the annual average stays ≥70%. Nevertheless, we recommend monthly tracking to avoid inadvertently consuming the grace margin:

- Strict accounting coding of revenue by category (eligible / ineligible)
- Monthly threshold checks, not just annual
- Proactive operational decisions (for example, re-invoicing certain revenue through a separate entity if the threshold is at risk)

## Stage 4: The mandatory annual audit

### The legal requirement

**All IT Park residents** — regardless of turnover, headcount, or asset size — undergo an **annual compliance check**, carried out by an audit entity, for the months in which the special regime applied.

### Purpose of the audit

The indicators checked are set each year by the MITP Administration together with the park Council (for 2026 — Decision no. 1 of 12.02.2026) and include:
- Registration in the Republic of Moldova as an entrepreneur
- Compliance with the 70% threshold for eligible-activity revenue
- Notification of employees about the social and health insurance regime and the tax on salaries

### Who can perform the audit

The resident **chooses and pays** the audit entity or auditor (from the list published by the MITP Administration). For IT Park residents, we recommend choosing auditors with specific experience in this regime — the checks are specialized and differ from a classic audit.

### Costs and deadlines

- **Audit cost**: starts at MDL 15,000–20,000 for small companies, going up to MDL 80,000+ for complex ones
- **Deadline**: the results of the check are submitted via the resident's profile on mitp.md no later than April 30 of the following year
- **Sanction for missing audit**: loss of resident status

## Maintaining resident status

### Periodic checks

The MITP Administration may carry out periodic checks of residents, either scheduled or risk-based. These checks focus on:

- Economic substance (real existence of employees, activities, clients)
- Conformity with declared activities
- Correctness of tax reporting
- Compliance with the law under which status was granted

### Communicating changes

Changes are communicated to the MITP Administration via the company's profile on mitp.md:

- **within 30 days**: name, administrator, address, founders, or ownership structure
- **within 10 days**: contact details and official e-mail addresses

## Exit scenarios and their consequences

### Voluntary withdrawal

A company may voluntarily withdraw from resident status through a written request to the MITP Administration. From the withdrawal date, the company reverts to the standard tax regime.

### Loss of status through non-compliance

If the 70% threshold is not met, the annual check is not performed, or other serious non-conformities arise, the resident may lose its status.

**Tax consequence**: if the resident does not meet the conditions for the incentives, its obligations to the budget are recalculated under the general regime, starting with the tax period in which the breach occurred (Law no. 77/2016 art. 15(5)) — the difference potentially reaching **millions of MDL retroactively**. This is, without question, the single largest risk of the IT Park regime.

### Naturally outgrowing the regime

For very large companies with very large turnover and high margins, the standard regime combined with complex tax planning **may become more advantageous than IT Park**. This transition must be analyzed professionally and is not, in practice, frequent.

## The most common mistakes

1. **Applying without genuinely verifying eligibility** — especially for companies with mixed activities, the precise calculation of the eligible proportion must be done before applying.

2. **Lack of accounting separation by category** — without proper accounting coding, the 70% threshold cannot be operationally monitored. See [the 5 most common LLC accounting mistakes](/en/blog/five-accounting-mistakes-srl-moldova-2026) for general context.

3. **Underestimating the per-employee threshold** — for small companies with modest revenue, the MDL 5,220 × employee minimum can render the regime no longer favorable.

4. **Postponing the annual check** — good auditors are crowded in March-April. Contracting in advance (November-December) is essential.

5. **Skipping employee notification** — notification about the single-tax regime is mandatory for existing employees before registration in the park and for new ones before hiring, and it is checked annually.

6. **Incorrect payroll records** — the practice of "hiding" salaries or operating undeclared payments is the fastest path to losing status. All payments to employees must be fully legal and transparent.

7. **Misunderstanding VAT** — VAT is not included in the single tax. Service exports are VAT-exempt with the right of deduction, but VAT registration remains mandatory once the threshold set by the Tax Code is exceeded.

**Pre-application:**
- ☐ Entity registered (LLC, JSC, or ÎI) with eligible CAEM-2 codes
- ☐ Existing employees notified about the single-tax regime
- ☐ Accounting configured with separation between eligible/ineligible activities
- ☐ Revenue structure projected with 70% threshold secured
- ☐ Bank accounts prepared for flow separation

**Application:**
- ☐ Complete documentation prepared
- ☐ Application submitted on the MITP platform with the administrator's electronic signature
- ☐ Resident contract signed within 30 days of approval; first membership fee paid within 5 working days

**Monthly operations:**
- ☐ Correct calculation of the single tax (max of 7% and the minimum threshold)
- ☐ Declaration and payment by the 25th of the following month
- ☐ Cumulative monitoring of the 70% threshold

- ☐ MITP membership fee paid by the 20th

**Annual operations:**
- ☐ Audit entity chosen in advance (November-December)
- ☐ Annual check results submitted by April 30
- ☐ Semi-annual reports submitted by July 25 and January 25
- ☐ Estimated revenue for the following year submitted by December 31
- ☐ Changes communicated within 30 days (contact details — within 10 days)

## Conclusion

Moldova IT Park is not, simply, a "reduced tax regime". It is a complete legislative construction, designed as an instrument of national competitiveness for the IT sector, with consistent tax benefits guaranteed by law and proportionate obligations.

For an eligible IT company, resident status represents **between a few hundred thousand and a few million MDL saved annually**. For an insufficiently configured company, the regime can become a trap — with retroactive reclassification risks that, in the most serious cases, exceed any savings obtained previously.

The difference between the two scenarios is not accidental. It is, without exception, the result of the quality of initial configuration and subsequent operational discipline. For companies intending to enter the regime, or for already-resident companies seeking to optimize compliance, professional assistance specialized in the IT Park regime is one of the few investments with a directly demonstrable tax return.
